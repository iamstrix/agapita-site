Agapita

A voice for stroke survivors who cannot speak

A research prototype helping people with Broca's aphasia communicate through drawing. The patient reviews and confirms each suggested message before sending it to their nurse.

  • Designed to work without internet access
  • Built around keeping patient information on site
  • Being developed for affordable tablets
See how it helps

Agapita is a prototype in development. It is not a medical device and has not yet been tested with patients.

The problem

After a stroke, many people understand everything around them but cannot get their words out.

This is called Broca's aphasia. Many people with it also have weakness on one side of the body, which makes fine movements hard.

Existing communication aids ask patients to tap through grids of small icons. That is slow and frustrating for someone with shaky hands. At night, with no one in the room, a patient may have no way to ask for help at all.

Agapita's goal is for the technology to adapt to the patient. Simple drawings, such as a circle, star or stick figure, are the starting point for a suggested message.

How it helps

The intended experience, currently being developed and still to be evaluated with patients and care teams.

1. The patient draws

A simple sketch on a bedside tablet with large buttons.

2. Review and confirm

Agapita suggests a meaning. The patient can choose a different one before sending.

3. The nurse receives

“I need my medication, please.”

Sent only after confirmation. The message can also be read aloud.

Illustrative example of the intended flow.

For patients

  • Communicate without speaking or spelling
  • Nothing is sent until the patient confirms it
  • Designed to support independence and dignity

For nurses and carers

  • Know what is needed before reaching the room
  • Less time spent guessing what a patient means
  • Aim to ease communication demands on busy shifts

For hospitals

  • No internet connection needed
  • Designed to keep patient information within the hospital
  • Affordable tablets instead of costly specialist devices

Intended impact

These are our design goals, not measured clinical or environmental outcomes. Patient testing has not yet taken place.

People

  • Support patient agency. Help stroke survivors express what they need and confirm their own messages.
  • Ease communication demands. Help nurses understand requests with less guesswork.
  • Widen access. Aim for an affordable option for public hospitals in the Philippines.

Environment

  • Explore device reuse. Assess whether compatible older tablets can serve as bedside communication aids.
  • Keep processing local. Develop offline operation without relying on cloud processing.
  • Explore reusable aids. Offer a digital option alongside existing communication materials.

Our story

Ten years ago, the founder's grandmother had a severe stroke. Years later she described the night shifts in hospital: no one in the room, no way to speak, waiting in silence until morning.

Agapita was built so that no patient has to wait like that again.

Giving a voice back to the beloved, one sketch at a time.
